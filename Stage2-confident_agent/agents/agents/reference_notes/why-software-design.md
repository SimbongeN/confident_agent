# Why Software Design Is Needed
keywords: software design, why design, purpose of software design, design before coding, client intimacy, requirements, maintenance cost, design definition
- Software design is the process of defining software methods, functions, objects and the overall structure and interaction of the code so that the resulting functionality satisfies users' requirements.
- Rushing into coding without requirements engineering and design leads to buggy, resource-hungry systems, rejected systems, expensive and hard development, long and unpredictable development times, and legacy systems that are almost impossible to change.
- Programmer myths: design is a waste of time; we must show the customer something quickly; we are judged by lines of code or person-months.
- Design helps meet system functional and non-functional requirements, prioritise (high wants vs just wants, must-haves) and make trade-offs between requirements of different priorities.
- Design communicates the high-level picture to all stakeholders; reducing detail makes large systems easier to grasp, and shows integration and interdependencies.
- Design decomposes the system into relatively independent sub-structures, enabling flexible distribution, clear chains of responsibility, partitioned development work and skill specialisation.
- Design reduces the cost of maintenance and upgrades over the life cycle, helps anticipate future changes, increases code reuse and eases integration with legacy and third-party systems.
- Systems should strive for client intimacy and operational excellence, fit the environment, adapt to technology changes and be scalable, agile and responsive.
