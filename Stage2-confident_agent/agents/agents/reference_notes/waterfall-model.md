# Waterfall model
keywords: waterfall, waterfall model, sequential development, linear model, phases, Royce, big design up front
- The waterfall model's phases are conception, requirements analysis, design, construction, testing, deployment and maintenance.
- Each phase is completed before the next begins, so progress flows largely in one direction.
- Advantage: it gives a structured approach with discrete, easy-to-explain phases and clear, identifiable milestones.
- Advantage: its emphasis on documentation helps new team members get up to speed and reduces knowledge loss when members leave.
- Advantage: problems caught early are cheaper to fix than those found later, by a factor of 50 to 200.
- Disadvantage: Royce said testing happening only at the end was "risky and [inviting] failure."
- Disadvantage: clients may not know their exact requirements until they see working software, which can force redesign, redevelopment, retesting and higher costs.
- Disadvantage: designers may not foresee future difficulties, and separating analysis from programming is hard because implementation exposes issues analysts missed.
- Winston W. Royce's 1970 article is often cited as having the first formal, detailed diagram of the process, though he described its flaws.
- The US Department of Defense adopted the model in DOD-STD-2167 in 1985 but later preferred against waterfall-type methods, starting with MIL-STD-498 in 1994.
