# Software development life cycle (SDLC)
keywords: SDLC, systems development life cycle, software development process, phases, methodology, waterfall, iterative, incremental, spiral, agile, V-model, prototyping, RAD
- The SDLC describes the typical phases a computer-based system passes through from inception to retirement.
- The SDLC does not prescribe how engineers do the work; prescriptive techniques are called methodologies, models, frameworks or formal processes.
- Phases described: conceptualization (feasibility study), requirements analysis, design, construction, acceptance (system testing), deployment, maintenance, and decommission.
- Phases can overlap and repeat, and the number and names of phases vary by source.
- A software development process is a set of prescribed steps that splits the work into smaller sub-processes and may define deliverables (artifacts).
- Waterfall is a sequential development approach in which development flows one way through the SDLC phases.
- Iterative models (e.g. RUP, DSDM) focus on stabilizing project scope and improving products through repeated cycles.
- Incremental development combines linear and iterative methods by breaking a project into smaller segments to reduce risk and ease change.
- The spiral model (Boehm, 1988) combines waterfall and rapid prototyping with deliberate iterative risk analysis, and suits large, complex systems.
- Agile is a group of frameworks based on iterative development in which requirements and solutions evolve through collaboration among self-organizing, cross-functional teams.
- Other models listed: prototyping, rapid application development (RAD), V-model (an extension of waterfall), and the Unified Process.
