# Design thinking
keywords: design thinking, empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test, human-centered design, five stages, IDEO, d.school
- Design thinking has five stages: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test.
- Empathize: research users' needs and gain insight into their experiences and motivations, setting aside the team's assumptions.
- Define: analyze research observations and state core user needs and problems as a human-centered problem statement.
- Ideate: challenge assumptions, view the problem from different angles and brainstorm innovative solutions.
- Prototype: build inexpensive, scaled-down versions of solutions to explore ideas and identify the best option.
- Test: evaluate prototypes with real users; results may lead the team to redefine the problem or revisit earlier stages.
- The stages are not strictly sequential; teams can run them in parallel, out of order, or repeat them.
- Benefit: fosters innovation and helps teams tackle ill-defined or unknown problems.
- Benefit: starts with user desirability, then weighs technical feasibility and business viability.
- Benefit: helps build products that meet customer needs, which can increase loyalty and give a competitive advantage.
- Wikipedia notes criticisms, e.g. that business uses promote a narrow view of design skills and that simplified versions struggle with highly uncertain problems.
